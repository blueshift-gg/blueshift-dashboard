# From Traditional Lending to DeFi Lending

When you apply for a mortgage, you hand over pay stubs, tax returns, and bank statements. A human reviews your application. If approved, a legal contract binds you to repay. If you default, courts and collections agencies pursue you.

DeFi lending does none of this. No documents, no identity, no courts.

Yet somehow, protocols like Aave and Compound have facilitated over $100 billion in loans without a single lawsuit. How?

The answer: **overcollateralization**. You want to borrow $7,500? First, deposit $10,000 as collateral. If you don't repay, the protocol keeps your collateral. No lawyers needed.

<ArticleSection name="How Traditional Lending Works" id="tradfi-lending" level="h2" />

### The Credit System

Traditional lending relies on trust, manufactured through:

**Identity verification**: Know Your Customer (KYC) confirms you are who you claim to be.

**Credit scores**: A number (300-850 in the US) summarizing your repayment history. Built over years, destroyed in months.

**Income verification**: Proof you can actually repay. Pay stubs, tax returns, employment letters.

**Legal contracts**: Enforceable agreements. Default, and courts can garnish wages, seize assets, destroy credit.

This system works for people with identity, history, and legal presence. It fails for:
- The 1.4 billion adults without bank accounts
- Anyone outside traditional employment
- Privacy-conscious individuals
- Cross-border transactions (which jurisdiction applies?)

### Bank Economics

Banks are intermediaries. They:
1. Collect deposits (paying 0.5% interest)
2. Lend deposits out (charging 7% interest)
3. Pocket the spread (6.5%)

The spread exists because banks provide services: credit assessment, legal enforcement, regulatory compliance. They deserve compensation.

But what if you could eliminate most of those services? What if code could replace credit officers, smart contracts could replace lawyers, and math could replace trust?

<ArticleSection name="The DeFi Solution" id="defi-solution" level="h2" />

### Overcollateralization: The Core Innovation

DeFi's answer to trustless lending is brutally simple: demand more collateral than the loan is worth.

```
Traditional loan:
  Income: $100,000/year
  Collateral: $0 (unsecured)
  Loan: $50,000
  Risk managed by: Credit score, legal system

DeFi loan:
  Income: Unknown
  Collateral: $10,000 in SOL
  Loan: $7,500 in USDC
  Risk managed by: Overcollateralization
```

If the borrower disappears, the protocol has $10,000 to cover a $7,500 debt. No courts required.

### Why Would Anyone Overcollateralize?

At first glance, this seems pointless. Why deposit $10,000 to borrow $7,500? You're net negative.

Common use cases:

**1. Leverage**: Deposit SOL, borrow USDC, buy more SOL. If SOL rises, you profit more than you would from just holding.

**2. Tax efficiency**: In many jurisdictions, borrowing isn't a taxable event. Selling is. Borrow against assets instead of selling them.

**3. Liquidity without selling**: You're long-term bullish on ETH but need cash now. Borrow against ETH, repay later, keep your ETH.

**4. Yield farming**: Deposit asset A, borrow asset B, deploy B in a yield farm earning more than the borrow rate.

**5. Short selling**: Borrow an asset you expect to decline, sell it, buy back cheaper, repay the loan.

<ArticleSection name="The Evolution" id="evolution" level="h2" />

### MakerDAO (2017): The Pioneer

MakerDAO introduced the first major DeFi lending system, but with a twist: you don't borrow existing assets. You mint new ones.

Deposit ETH, mint DAI (a stablecoin). Your ETH is locked in a "Vault." If ETH's price drops too far, your vault gets liquidated.

```
MakerDAO Model:
  Deposit: 10 ETH ($20,000)
  Mint: Up to 13,333 DAI (150% collateralization minimum)
  
  If ETH drops 25%:
    Collateral value: $15,000
    Collateralization: 112% (below 150%)
    Result: Liquidation
```

MakerDAO created money (DAI) backed by crypto collateral. Revolutionary, but limited: only one asset (ETH initially), only one thing to borrow (DAI).

### Compound (2018): The Money Market

Compound generalized the model into a **money market**: anyone can deposit any supported asset and earn interest. Anyone can borrow any supported asset by posting collateral.

Key innovations:

**cTokens**: When you deposit USDC, you receive cUSDC. These "cTokens" represent your share of the pool and automatically accrue interest.

**Utilization-based rates**: Interest rates adjust based on supply and demand. High borrowing = high rates. Low borrowing = low rates.

**Multi-asset collateral**: Deposit ETH, borrow USDC. Or deposit USDC, borrow ETH. Any combination works.

```rust
// The Compound model: shares, not balances
pub struct CToken {
    pub underlying: Pubkey,        // USDC, ETH, etc.
    pub total_supply: u64,         // Total cTokens minted
    pub total_cash: u64,           // Underlying in contract
    pub total_borrows: u64,        // Underlying borrowed out
    pub borrow_index: u128,        // Interest accumulator
}

// Your cTokens grow in value as interest accrues
fn exchange_rate(market: &CToken) -> u128 {
    if market.total_supply == 0 {
        return INITIAL_EXCHANGE_RATE;
    }
    (market.total_cash + market.total_borrows) / market.total_supply
}
```

### Aave (2020): Refinement and Features

Aave built on Compound's foundation with additional features:

**Flash loans**: Borrow any amount without collateral, as long as you repay in the same transaction. Enables arbitrage, liquidations, and collateral swaps.

**Stable rates**: Lock in a borrow rate instead of floating with utilization. Predictability for borrowers.

**Credit delegation**: Let someone else borrow against your collateral. Enables undercollateralized lending through social trust.

**Efficiency mode (E-Mode)**: Higher LTV for correlated assets. If you deposit stETH and borrow ETH, the price correlation justifies higher leverage.

<ArticleSection name="The Core Mechanics" id="core-mechanics" level="h2" />

### Share-Based Accounting

Imagine tracking every depositor's balance individually. Alice deposits 1000 USDC. Bob deposits 500. Interest accrues every second. You'd need to update Alice's balance, Bob's balance, and everyone else's. Every block. Forever.

This is computationally expensive. Instead, we use **shares**.

You deposit USDC, you get shares (often called "aTokens" or "cTokens"). Shares represent your percentage of the pool, not a fixed amount. When interest accrues, the pool grows, but the share count stays the same. Your shares are now worth more.

```
Day 1: Pool has 10,000 USDC, 10,000 shares
       You deposit 1,000 USDC → receive 1,000 shares (10% of pool)

Day 30: Interest accrues, pool now has 11,000 USDC, still 10,000 shares
        Your 1,000 shares = 10% of 11,000 = 1,100 USDC
```

One global update (the pool total) instead of thousands of individual updates.

```rust
pub fn deposit(ctx: Context<Deposit>, amount: u64) -> Result<()> {
    let pool = &mut ctx.accounts.pool;
    
    // Calculate shares: your_deposit / total_assets * total_shares
    let shares = if pool.total_shares == 0 {
        amount  // First depositor: 1:1
    } else {
        (amount as u128)
            .checked_mul(pool.total_shares as u128)
            .unwrap()
            .checked_div(pool.total_assets() as u128)
            .unwrap() as u64
    };
    
    // Transfer underlying and mint shares
    transfer_tokens(ctx.accounts.user_token, ctx.accounts.pool_vault, amount)?;
    mint_shares(ctx.accounts.user_shares, shares)?;
    
    pool.total_deposits += amount;
    pool.total_shares += shares;
    
    Ok(())
}
```

### The Borrow Index

Interest accrues continuously, but we can't update every borrower's debt every block. Instead, we use a global **borrow index**.

The index starts at 1.0 and grows as interest accrues. When you borrow, we record the current index. Your debt is always:

```
current_debt = principal × (current_index / index_at_borrow)
```

If the index was 1.0 when you borrowed 1000 USDC, and it's now 1.05, you owe 1050 USDC.

```rust
#[account]
pub struct BorrowPosition {
    pub principal: u64,           // Original borrow amount
    pub index_at_borrow: u128,    // Borrow index when position opened
}

#[account]
pub struct LendingPool {
    pub borrow_index: u128,       // Global interest accumulator
    pub last_update: i64,         // Last accrual timestamp
    pub total_borrows: u64,       // Total borrowed (with interest)
}

pub fn get_current_debt(position: &BorrowPosition, pool: &LendingPool) -> u64 {
    (position.principal as u128)
        .checked_mul(pool.borrow_index)
        .unwrap()
        .checked_div(position.index_at_borrow)
        .unwrap() as u64
}

pub fn accrue_interest(pool: &mut LendingPool, current_time: i64) {
    let elapsed = current_time - pool.last_update;
    if elapsed <= 0 {
        return;
    }
    
    let rate = calculate_borrow_rate(pool);
    let interest_factor = PRECISION + (rate * elapsed as u128 / SECONDS_PER_YEAR);
    
    pool.borrow_index = pool.borrow_index * interest_factor / PRECISION;
    pool.total_borrows = (pool.total_borrows as u128 * interest_factor / PRECISION) as u64;
    pool.last_update = current_time;
}
```

<ArticleSection name="Why Solana Changes Things" id="solana" level="h2" />

### The Account Model

Ethereum's lending protocols store all user positions in a single contract. This works because Ethereum processes transactions sequentially.

Solana processes transactions in parallel. If two users interact with the same account, they serialize. To maximize throughput, Solana programs use separate accounts for each user's position.

```rust
// Ethereum-style (doesn't parallelize well)
mapping(address => UserPosition) positions;

// Solana-style (parallelizes)
#[account]
pub struct UserPosition {
    pub owner: Pubkey,
    pub collateral: u64,
    pub borrowed: u64,
    pub borrow_index: u128,
}
// Each user has their own account
```

### Implications for Design

**Account creation**: Users must create position accounts before borrowing. This costs rent (small SOL deposit).

**Account limits**: Each account has size limits (~10KB typically). Complex positions may need multiple accounts.

**Cross-program invocation (CPI)**: Liquidation bots need to call your program. Design clean interfaces.

**Oracle integration**: Solana oracles (Pyth, Switchboard) update prices in separate accounts. Your program reads these accounts.

```rust
#[derive(Accounts)]
pub struct Borrow<'info> {
    #[account(mut)]
    pub pool: Account<'info, LendingPool>,
    
    #[account(
        init_if_needed,
        payer = borrower,
        space = 8 + UserPosition::INIT_SPACE,
        seeds = [b"position", pool.key().as_ref(), borrower.key().as_ref()],
        bump
    )]
    pub position: Account<'info, UserPosition>,
    
    #[account(mut)]
    pub borrower: Signer<'info>,
    
    /// Price feed for collateral asset
    pub collateral_oracle: Account<'info, PriceAccount>,
    
    /// Price feed for borrowed asset  
    pub borrow_oracle: Account<'info, PriceAccount>,
    
    pub system_program: Program<'info, System>,
}
```

<ArticleSection name="The Risk Spectrum" id="risk-spectrum" level="h2" />

### Protocol Risk

Every lending protocol can fail:

**Smart contract bugs**: Code has vulnerabilities. Euler lost $197M in 2023 to a logic error.

**Oracle manipulation**: Bad price data enables bad debt. Mango Markets lost $114M to oracle manipulation.

**Governance attacks**: If governance can change parameters, attackers can too. Beanstalk lost $182M to a flash loan governance attack.

**Economic attacks**: Legitimate but extractive behavior. Short-term profit for attackers, losses for LPs.

### User Risk

Borrowers face unique risks:

**Liquidation**: If collateral value drops, you lose a portion to liquidators (typically 5-15% bonus).

**Interest rate spikes**: During high utilization, borrow rates can exceed 100% APY.

**Oracle delays**: Prices update in blocks. Fast-moving markets can liquidate you before you react.

### Depositor Risk

Lenders aren't risk-free:

**Utilization lock**: At 100% utilization, you can't withdraw. You're stuck until borrowers repay.

**Bad debt socialization**: If liquidations fail, depositors may absorb losses.

**Smart contract risk**: Your deposits live in code. Bugs affect you.

**Aave**: Largest by TVL, multi-chain, battle-tested. Conservative parameters, slow to add assets.
