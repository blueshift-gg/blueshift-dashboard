# Conclusion

You understand formal verification for Solana programs: what bounded model checking proves, how to write proof harnesses with Kani, how to interpret counterexamples, and how to apply verification to real on-chain arithmetic and state transitions.

<ArticleSection name="Complementary Tools in Practice" id="complementary-tools" level="h2" />

A lending protocol with a liquidation function illustrates why no single tool is sufficient:

- **Manual review** catches that the liquidation bonus formula gives an unfair advantage to liquidators, creating an economic incentive for self-liquidation. No automated tool flags this because the code is technically correct; the economics are wrong.
- **Fuzzing** discovers that calling `liquidate` with a specific sequence of `deposit`, `borrow`, and `update_oracle` instructions causes the health factor calculation to underflow. No reviewer anticipated this 4-step sequence.
- **Formal verification** proves that the collateral transfer arithmetic never overflows for any valid position size, and that the health factor calculation is monotonically decreasing with respect to borrowed amount.

Each finding is critical. Each was invisible to the other tools.

<ArticleSection name="Applying What You Have Learned" id="applying-knowledge" level="h2" />

For your own programs and audits, a practical workflow:

1. **Write the program** with checked arithmetic from the start. Use `checked_add`, `checked_mul`, and `checked_div` everywhere.

2. **Add Kani proofs** for critical arithmetic functions during development, not after. Verify that your share calculations never overflow, that your fee computations are monotonic, and that your state transitions preserve conservation invariants.

3. **Write Trident fuzz targets** for instruction-level behavior. Focus invariants on state consistency, token conservation, and access control. Run fuzzing in CI on every PR.

4. **Get a manual review** before deployment. Share your verification results and fuzz coverage with reviewers so they can focus on logic correctness, economic design, and integration risks.

5. **Continue fuzzing in production**. Run extended fuzzing campaigns against deployed code. The corpus grows over time, and new mutations explore deeper state space.

The tools exist and the techniques are accessible. A Kani proof harness is 10 lines of Rust. A Trident fuzz target is a few hundred lines. A thorough manual review follows a systematic checklist.
